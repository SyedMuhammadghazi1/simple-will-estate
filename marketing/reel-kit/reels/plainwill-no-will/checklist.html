Before you write a will: three decisions

If you don't have a will, your state's default rules decide who gets your things, and a judge decides who raises your children if no parent is left. A will is how you put your own answers on paper. These are the decisions to make first.

Decision 1

Who gets what

  • The people (or charities) you want to receive your things
  • Anything specific going to someone specific: a ring, the car
  • Who gets "everything else"
  • A backup if your first choice can't inherit
Decision 2

Who you'd name for your kids

  • The person you'd want to raise your minor children
  • A backup, in case they can't
  • Ask them first: it is a big thing to say yes to

A will lets you name your choice. How a guardian is appointed or confirmed depends on your state, and a surviving parent normally keeps custody.

Decision 3

Who's in charge

  • Your executor: the person who carries out the will
  • Someone organized, whom you trust with paperwork and money
  • A backup, and a conversation so it isn't a surprise
Then

Sign it properly

  • In most states: in writing, signed by you, with two adult witnesses
  • Signing rules vary by state: check yours before you sign
  • An unsigned will has no legal effect
  • Tell your executor where the signed original is kept
Good to know

What a will doesn't do on its own

  • Accounts with a named beneficiary (retirement plans, life insurance) usually follow the form on file, not the will: check those forms are current
  • Jointly owned property often passes to the other owner
  • Revisit after a marriage, a divorce, a new child or a move to another state
  • Talk to an attorney if you own a business, have a beneficiary with special needs, expect a dispute, hold property abroad, or have a large or complicated estate
General information, not legal advice. Plainwill is not a law firm and is not a substitute for the advice of an attorney. Laws vary by state, and this checklist does not cover every situation. Louisiana follows different rules for wills.
